About

I am a computer scientist who came to code through literature. After a B.A. in Modern Humanities and an M.Sc. in Digital Humanities at the University of Catania, where I built a digital library of Sicilian and co-developed DanteGPT, I started a PhD in Computer Science in December 2024, supervised by Prof. Emiliano Alessio Tramontana and co-funded by the National Centre ICSC. Reading texts closely taught me to read places the same way. Today I train vision models on aerial images of Sicily, turn Christopher Alexander's urban patterns into rules computed on OpenStreetMap data and, as a visiting PhD researcher at IDLab in Ghent, look for the road features that make cycling races dangerous.

Research interests

Outside the lab: karate, black belt 3rd dan, athlete and instructor for children.

Since 2025

Aerial and satellite imagery

Computer vision and deep learning, object detection and segmentation, on real aerial and satellite images of Sicily, on a dataset I built: by May 2026, 605 images, 3,926 annotated instances and 11 categories. A hybrid YOLO-C3 pipeline, a pixel-based classifier plus YOLO, designed for drones. And a study of what LLMs can and cannot see in aerial images.

  • YOLO-based recognition of crop categories, IEEE WETICE 2025
  • When LLMs assist (and when not), Future Internet 2026
  • YOLO-C3 hybrid approach, Future Internet 2026

Since 2026

Urban pattern analysis

Urban computing software that translates Christopher Alexander's design patterns into computable rules on open geospatial data, for the automatic evaluation of urban areas. Pattern 11, Local Transport Areas, became seven measurable rules, applied to 23 cities in City Explorer. In progress: the shape of junctions, T versus X, read from aerial images for Pattern 50, T Junctions.

  • City Explorer, the 23 cities on a map
  • Seven rules for a Local Transport Area, IEEE WETICE 2026

Ghent, 2026

Sport data science: cycling safety

As a visiting PhD researcher at IDLab, Ghent University, with Prof. Steven Verstockt: the safety of cycling races, in the line the group develops in collaboration with the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). AI models that automatically find the dangerous points of race routes.

Work in progress, not yet published.
